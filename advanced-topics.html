Force Field Selection for Ionic Liquids

The force field is the single most important choice for IL simulation quality. No universal FF exists; selection depends on your IL class and target properties.

📋 Force Field Comparison Table
FF FamilyCharge ModelBest ForCaveats
CL&P / OPLS-AAFull integer charges (±1)Imidazolium, pyrrolidinium ILsOverstructured; high viscosity
CL&P scaled (0.8e)Scaled charges (±0.8)Transport propertiesAd hoc scaling; less transferable
AMOEBA / Drude polarizableInduced dipolesInterfacial, mixed systems10–100× cost; parameter scarcity
GAFF/GAFF2 + RESPQM-derivedNovel/asymmetric ionsRequires QM per ion; validation needed
Coarse-grained (MARTINI, etc.)Effective beadsLarge scale, long time (μs–ms)Loses atomistic detail; property-specific

Key principle: Validate your FF against experimental density, viscosity, and conductivity before computing novel properties. A 5% density error often means 2–3× viscosity error.

Structural Analysis: Beyond RDFs

Standard radial distribution functions (RDFs) are necessary but insufficient for ILs.

🔍 Essential Analysis Types
  • Cation–anion RDF (center-of-mass): First peak position → contact distance; coordination number from integral.
  • Cation–cation / anion–anion RDFs: Reveal like-charge ordering (should be repulsive at short range).
  • Spatial distribution functions (SDF): 3D maps of anion around cation (and vice versa). Use gmx spatial or VMD VolMap.
  • Domain analysis: Identify polar/apolar nanodomains via clustering (e.g., alkyl tail aggregation).
  • Orientation correlation: Angle between cation ring plane and anion approach vector.
💻 GROMACS Commands for Coordination Number 
# GROMACS: coordination number from RDF
gmx rdf -f prod.xtc -s prod.tpr -n index.ndx -o rdf_cation_anion.xvg
# Select cation COM as ref, anion COM as sel
# Coordination number = 4πρ ∫0rmin g(r) r² dr

Advanced: Polarizable Force Fields

For systems where electronic polarization matters (interfaces, mixtures with water, electric fields):

🔬 Polarizable FF Options
  • Drude oscillators: Massless charged particles attached to atoms via harmonic springs. Implemented in CHARMM, NAMD, OpenMM.
  • AMOEBA: Multipole-based with induced dipoles (Thole damping). Tinker, OpenMM.
  • Cost: 5–20× standard MD. Requires dual thermostats (real atoms + Drude particles at 1 K).

When to use: computing dielectric constants, electrode interfaces, or when fixed-charge FFs fail qualitatively (e.g., IL/water miscibility).
