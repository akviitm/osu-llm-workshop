System Preparation

Building an ionic liquid simulation box requires careful attention to charge neutrality and initial packing:

🔧 Step-by-Step Workflow
  1. Generate ion pairs: Start with a single cation–anion pair at a reasonable separation (0.5–0.7 nm).
  2. Replicate: Use gmx genconf or packmol to create a box with ~500–2000 ion pairs.
  3. Energy minimize: Steepest descent until max force < 100 kJ/mol/nm.
  4. Equilibrate: NVT (1–5 ns) then NPT (5–20 ns) at target T and P.
💻 Example GROMACS Commands 
# Example GROMACS workflow
gmx grompp -f minim.mdp -c il_box.gro -p topol.top -o min.tpr
gmx mdrun -deffnm min
gmx grompp -f nvt.mdp -c min.gro -p topol.top -o nvt.tpr
gmx mdrun -deffnm nvt
gmx grompp -f npt.mdp -c nvt.gro -p topol.top -o npt.tpr
gmx mdrun -deffnm npt

Electrostatics: The Critical Choice

For ionic liquids, electrostatic treatment dominates accuracy and cost:

📊 Electrostatics Method Comparison
Method Cost Suitability for ILs
Cutoff (plain) Low ❌ Unacceptable—misses long-range order
Reaction field Low ⚠️ Poor for charge-ordered systems
PME (Particle Mesh Ewald) Medium ✅ Standard choice
PPPM / Ewald High ✅ Accurate, for validation

For PME: use a real-space cutoff of 1.0–1.2 nm, Fourier spacing ≤ 0.12 nm, and interpolation order 4–6. Verify energy conservation in NVE after equilibration.

Thermostats and Barostats

Ionic liquids have slow momentum and energy relaxation. Recommended choices:

🌡️ Thermostat & Barostat Settings
  • Thermostat: Nosé–Hoover chains (NHC) with τT = 1–2 ps. Avoid velocity rescaling (Berendsen) for production—it suppresses fluctuations.
  • Barostat: Parrinello–Rahman with τP = 5–10 ps, compressibility 4.5×10−5 bar−1 (typical for ILs). Isotropic for bulk; semi-isotropic if studying interfaces.

For NVT equilibration before NPT, a weaker coupling (τT = 0.5 ps) helps reach target T quickly.

Time Step and Constraints

⏱️ Time Step Recommendations
  • Time step: 2 fs with constraints = h-bonds (LINCS/SETTLE). For flexible IL models with explicit H, 1 fs may be needed.
  • Constraints: Constrain all bonds involving hydrogen. For rigid ion models (e.g., some coarse-grained), constrain all bonds.
  • LINCS order: 4–6; iterations = 1 (standard) or 2 (high accuracy).

Convergence and Sampling

Typical production runs for ionic liquids:

📈 Recommended Run Lengths
  • Structural properties (RDFs, density): 20–50 ns
  • Dynamical properties (diffusion, conductivity): 100–500 ns
  • Slow processes (domain rearrangement, glassy dynamics): >1 μs

Run multiple independent replicas (3–5) with different initial velocities to assess statistical uncertainty.
